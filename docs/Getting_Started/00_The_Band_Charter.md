# Before You Hit the Road: The Band Charter

Every touring band lives inside a tricky paradox: **a band is both a business and a family.**

It is a formal organization that signs venue contracts, buys €2,000 merch runs, rents vans, and files taxes—and at the same time, it is a tight-knit group of friends sharing a rehearsal room, splitting highway tolls, and living out of each other's pockets on tour.

When bands break up or burn out, it is rarely because of creative differences. More often, it is because of unspoken money resentment. And that resentment usually starts right at the beginning, when everyone assumes they are on the same page without ever writing the rules down.

Before you log your first tank of gas or sell your first T-shirt, the smartest thing your band can do is sit down—sober, away from a noisy bar after rehearsal—and write a **Band Charter**.

---

## 1. Agree on Fairness, Contributions, and Goals in Writing

A **Band Charter** doesn't need to be a 20-page legal contract. It is a clear, one-page written agreement that spells out:
* What your band is trying to achieve (weekend hobby vs. touring business).
* What each member can realistically contribute in time and money.
* How group expenses and profits are divided.

Every band can tailor its charter to fit its own reality. That said, if you want to get the most out of BandMath—and keep group politics to zero—**the cleanest model is simple: everybody agrees to share all group expenses and all band profits equally (`1/N`).**

*(What if one bandmate genuinely can't afford to pitch in upfront for a new merch print run? BandMath supports that too—you can exclude individual members from merch profit splits inside your band roster while still splitting shared travel, van, and rehearsal costs equally.)*

---

## 2. Joining an Ecosystem of Trust & Attribution

When you start using BandMath, you aren't just downloading an expense calculator—you are opting into an ecosystem where **every single euro is tracked, photographed, and attributed**.

That transparency removes guesswork, **but it only works when paired with mutual trust**. When four or five people share financial resources, everyone has to trust that everyone else will spend group money sensibly. Your Band Charter is what turns unspoken assumptions into shared guardrails.

### Define What Is—and Isn't—a Shared Expense
Write down two explicit lists in your charter before your first tour:

| Shared Band Expenses (Group Splits) | Personal Consumables (Individual Cost) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Van rental, tour fuel, highway tolls, parking | Guitar strings, picks, cables, and drum sticks |
| Rehearsal room rent & studio tracking sessions | Personal instrument repairs & pedalboard gear |
| Merch printing runs, shipping, & promo posters | Energy drinks, coffee, and snacks on the drive |
| Shared PA/backline repairs & hired session crew | Personal meals & bar tabs outside band per diems |

If it is on the **Personal Consumables** side, you buy it yourself—no receipts, no group debate.

### Set Autonomy Thresholds & Approval Rules
You should never have to hold a group vote in a parking garage:
* **Everyday Autonomy Threshold:** Agree on a small pre-approved limit (for example, up to `€30`) for obvious road essentials like highway tolls, parking meters, or a roll of gaff tape. Anyone can pay and log it without asking first.
* **Obvious Tour Necessities:** If someone is standing at the rental counter picking up the tour van or filling the tank on the highway, it is understood the group covers it.
* **Major Purchases (`3/4` or Unanimous Sign-Off):** For anything outside the pre-approved list or above your threshold (like a `€1,500` vinyl pressing, a festival buyout, or a new merch run), require unanimous agreement—or an agreed majority like `3/4`—*before* anyone swipes a card.

## 3. Why "Pay First, Split in the App" Beats a Pooled Band Fund

A common question from new bands is: *"Shouldn't we just have everyone pitch `€100` a month into a shared band pot first?"*

In practice, pooling cash upfront creates more friction than it solves. **Waiting to buy until you need something—and letting one person pay and log it—removes an entire step of resistance:**

1. **Zero Premature Friction:** With a pooled band fund, everyone has to agree to hand over cash *before* they even know what it's being spent on. When bandmates have different day-job cash flow from week to week, forced monthly buy-ins breed hesitation and quiet stress.
2. **Natural Balancing Over Time:** In BandMath, you wait until the band actually needs to rent the van or print the shirts. One person covers it, snaps the receipt into **[The Ledger](../Features/01_The_Ledger.md)**, and everyone's share updates automatically. Next week, another bandmate covers fuel and hotel deposits—so balances often even out organically without cash constantly changing hands.
3. **Settle Up Less Often:** Because BandMath tracks every cent down to the last penny (via **[The Penny Catcher Algorithm](../Features/06_The_Penny_Catcher.md)**), you don't have to settle up after every single rehearsal. Your Band Charter can state that the band settles **[Standings](../Features/01_The_Ledger.md#automated-debt-settlement)** once a month, at the end of each tour, or whenever any single member's balance crosses an agreed threshold (say `±€150`).

## 4. Why Bother When You Already Have a Spreadsheet?

Nobody wants "one more app" just for the sake of having another app on their phone. If a spreadsheet already feels good enough, why switch?

1. **It Takes the Pressure Off Your Group Dynamic:** Spreadsheets fail on tour. Receipts get crumpled in pockets, formulas break at 2 AM, one person gets stuck acting as the unpaid band accountant, and small mistakes turn into awkward conversations. BandMath automates your internal accounting—receipts, merch inventory COGS, tax set-asides, and minimum-transfer debt settlement—so money stays boring and friendships stay intact.
2. **The Same Pattern Scales to Venues, Promoters & Tour Managers:** BandMath doesn't stop at your band's internal van fund. When you walk into a club at the end of the night, **[The Closing Room](../Events_and_Closing_Room/04_Multi_Band_Closing_Room.md)** applies the exact same transparent philosophy across *external* organizations—letting the Venue, Promoter, Tour Manager, and every Band on the bill split shared ticket pools, door cash, and show expenses cleanly with zero spreadsheets.
